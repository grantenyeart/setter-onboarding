Your one job: book the Zoom

You are not closing on the phone. You open, find the pain, match a product, and book a Zoom for the closer. That is the whole game.

Keep your head up at the goal. Like soccer, you do not stare at your feet doing skill moves. Every call, eyes on the Zoom.

The Call Map

Every call walks this path

1 Open with the hook
2 Get the number, then read it: room, or slammed?
3 Diagnose the pain (ask a ton of questions)
4 Match the pain to the product
5 Handle the objection
6 Book the Zoom
Talk track

The opener tells you which road you are on in the first 10 seconds. Nail that and the rest of the call writes itself.

Step 1 · The Opener

Same four lines, every call

The opener
"Hey, is this [Company]?"

"Perfect. I was wondering, do you guys do ceramic coatings, or just detailing?"

"Okay, perfect. Well, I was actually calling because I'm looking to partner up with someone in the area to bring in more work. And honestly, it's really hard to find good detailers in your area, and you guys stood out to me."

"How many more ceramic coatings could you take on per week?"  or  "How many more details could you take on per week?"
Why it works

The ceramic question is not small talk. It is the cheapest qualifying question on the call, and it picks the product, the price and the word you use for the rest of the conversation. Then "partner up" instead of "sell you," and "it's hard to find good detailers in your area and you guys stood out" tells them they were chosen, not dialed. Ask for the number using their word.

Step 2 · Read The Number

Their number picks the road

They give you a number
They need WORK. "Perfect, that is exactly why I called. I bring detailers 8 to 15 details a week, just checking who can handle it." -> Lead Gen or Pay Per Click.
"None, I'm slammed"
Do NOT deflate. Get excited. "Two weeks? My man, you are killing it!" They are drowning in calls. -> AI Receptionist or DGS.
Talk track

No number, no diagnosis. And "booked up" is not a no, it is a different pitch. A slammed shop is losing calls it cannot answer. That is your opening, not the end of the call.

Step 3 · Diagnose Like A Doctor

Ask, then say the pain out loud

1
"Where do you get most of your jobs? Word of mouth, Google, Facebook?"
2
"Are you running any marketing right now, or have you before?"
3
"Are you the one answering calls AND doing the detailing?"
4
"What time do you close? After hours, does it go to voicemail?"
Talk track

A doctor finds the pain before writing the script. Ask, listen, then repeat their pain back to them with a number on it so they FEEL it.

The Missed-Call Math

Make them feel the leak

Walk them up the ladder
"How many calls a day? ... Are you the one detailing AND answering? ... After hours, straight to voicemail? ... How many you missing a week? ... And your average detail runs about $250?"
Then hit them with it

Detailing shops miss about 1 in 3 calls. "10 missed calls a week at $250 is $2,500 a week walking out the door. If I could make sure you never miss one again, would that be worth a quick look?" Say the number LOUD.

Step 4 · Match The Product

Pain to product

"I want more work / not enough jobs"
Lead Gen (rank #1 on Google)
"Booked solid, phone always ringing, missing calls"
AI Receptionist
"No website, or no way to book online"
Website
"Not tracking leads, no follow-up, slow in winter"
Detailer Growth System
"Wants more high-ticket ceramic jobs"
Meta / Facebook ads
Talk track

Pitch the ONE that matches their biggest pain. That is why you diagnose first. Dumping all five overwhelms them and loses the booking.

Product · Lead Gen (Google Ads)

The door-opener

The pitch
"We rank your company number one on Google and bring you 8 to 15 details a week. No money up front, fully performance based, and we cover the ad spend."
The price (pattern interrupt)
"How much? That is the best part, you ready? Only $30 per qualified lead. Flat. A $250 detail or a $1,000 ceramic, still just $30."
Talk track

This is the ONLY product where you say the price on the phone, because it sells itself. It is almost a no-brainer. State it proud, then go to the Zoom.

Product · AI Receptionist

Sell the freedom

The pitch
"It answers every call in your own voice, hyper realistic. Two ways: full 24/7 receptionist, or just a backup that catches your missed calls so they never go to another detailer."
"AI is garbage" objection
"That is exactly what everyone says before they hear it. Let me show you on the Zoom, it will blow your mind. Worst case, we make a friend."
Talk track

The backup (missed-calls-only) is the easy yes almost nobody turns down. Sell the freedom of never touching the phone, not the tech.

Talk track

Step 5 · The Objections

Handle it, then steer to the Zoom

Price (anything but Lead Gen)
"Like pricing a car I have not seen, it depends what you need. Let us hop on the Zoom."
"I need to think about it"
"Totally. Is it money or trust? Do you think this fixes the problem we just talked about?"
"I get these calls all the time"
"I bet you do. But I doubt you have heard a model like this. No money up front..."
"Just send me info"
"Easiest is a quick Zoom, worst case we make a friend. Today or tomorrow?"
"How do I know it works?"
"We do it for detailers across the US. We will SHOW you for two weeks first."
Talk track

Every answer ends pointing back at the Zoom. The objection is never the enemy. The un-booked call is.

Step 6 · The Close

Book the Zoom with the closer

The line
"Let us hop on a quick Zoom with our closer so you can see exactly how it works and if you are a good fit for us. I am not asking for your card, worst case we make a friend."
1
Never yes/no. Always this-or-that: "Today or tomorrow?" then "Morning or evening?"
2
Get their last name and best email, then book it on the Rapid Reach page.
3
Flip the ego: "see if you are a good fit for US." The pressure is on them, not you.
The Secret Weapon

Tonality wins the call

Do this
Use their name, a lot. Compliment them ("you are killing it"). Be on their team. Be lighthearted, crack a joke, mirror how they talk.
Paint the picture
"So you are out there with the vacuum in one hand and the phone in the other?" Tell stories: "You sound just like our Minnesota client before he switched."
Talk track

People love hearing their own name and hearing about themselves. Sound like a friend calling a friend, not a telemarketer reading a card.
